01 / Context

Learning the acoustic response.

A compact regression problem with a measurable engineering outcome.

The problem

Airflow produces sound.

Airfoil self-noise varies with geometry, flow conditions and frequency. A single linear relationship may not capture the patterns in the measured response.

The objective

Estimate from five inputs.

Use experimental observations to predict scaled sound pressure level, then compare a linear baseline with a model that can capture nonlinear interactions.

The use case

Explore measured conditions.

Demonstrate a data-driven surrogate for acoustic analysis. This study supports model comparison and exploration within the dataset, with external validation left for future work.

03 / Method

From measurements to predictions.

A modular workflow with a shared split and scaler for a fair baseline comparison.

  1. 01Dataset & EDALoad · inspect · validate
  2. 02Split & preprocess80/20 · training-fit scaler
  3. 03Linear baselineOrdinary least squares
  4. 04Tuned XGBoost54 settings · 5-fold CV
  5. 05Evaluate & predictHoldout · saved artifacts

Training: 1,202 observations. Grid search minimizes cross-validation mean squared error. XGBoost is triggered when baseline R² is below 0.85.

Final estimator: 300 trees, depth 7, learning rate 0.1, subsample 0.8. These parameters were inspected directly from the saved model.

Evaluation protocol and reproducibility notes

The website reconstructs the 301-row holdout with train_test_split(test_size=0.2, random_state=42) and applies the saved scaler. Its mean, variance and sample count match the reconstructed training set. No model is retrained to generate these results.

The original comparison notebook evaluates all rows, including training data. Its charts are not used as holdout evidence here. Scaling is fitted before cross-validation in the original trainer; future work should put preprocessing inside each CV fold. The repository has no original split manifest or complete training log.

06 / Interpretability

What the trees use.

Displacement thickness and chord length have the highest normalized average gain in this saved XGBoost model, followed by frequency.

Gain measures improvement in the training objective at splits. It shows neither effect direction nor causation. Correlated features can share or redistribute importance; this is not a SHAP analysis.

09 / Scope & next steps

Good results have boundaries.

The verified result is specific to this dataset, saved estimator and random split.

Current limitations

A small, shared experimental domain.

Only 1,503 measurements and two model families are considered. Random train/test rows can share aerodynamic configurations, so the score does not establish generalization to new test conditions or other airfoils.

No external validation, uncertainty estimation or causal analysis is implemented. Existing physical checks print warnings rather than reject invalid data.

Future work · not yet implemented

Test transfer and quantify uncertainty.

  • Group validation by experimental condition.
  • Fit preprocessing inside each cross-validation fold.
  • Add permutation importance or SHAP analysis.
  • Evaluate calibrated prediction intervals.
  • Package and deploy a versioned inference API.
